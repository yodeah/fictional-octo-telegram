The bout

Two fighters duel until one reaches 0 HP. Fights are fully idle: you prepare beforehand, then only watch. There is no time limit.

  • Before a bout: pin any gear slot, or leave it on Auto to keep what the last matchup chose.
  • During a bout: no interaction. Gear, Reset, matchups, the venue and scene markers are locked.
  • Playback: pause, or run at 1×, 2× or 4×. This changes how you watch, never the fight.
  • Rematch, Auto-rematch and Reset: the same two fighters, levels and stats. Pinned gear changes apply.
  • Generate matchup: two new builds, no more than nine levels apart, with all gear rerolled. Builds are vanguard, duelist, spellblade, marksman, outrider, arcanist and battlemage.
  • Arena: the Sand Pit, Fernwood Clearing and Moonlit Ruins rotate every one to three bouts. They are cosmetic, including the crowd and wildlife you can click between bouts.
  • Record: win totals last until the page is closed or reloaded.

How fighters decide

When free, a fighter scores each option and takes the best one. The score weighs damage, the target’s armor, skill, speed, reach, energy, and a penalty for repeating the same weapon.

  • Support spells come first when needed: heal at 61% HP or less, ward at 77% or less, Blood Pact at 24 mana or less (with at least 41% HP), Tailwind at 40 energy or less.
  • Without a staff in hand, attack spells are only considered some of the time. A staff can always cast, with a free Staff Spark as a fallback. Staves never swing in melee.
  • Fighters press crossbow users at close range. A crowded shot loses up to 53% damage and much of its accuracy.
  • Fighters move and act independently, so attacks can overlap. They hold back while the opponent’s shield is raised against them.

Hits and damage

  • Melee: the moving weapon has to touch the shield or body. Head hits deal 124%, body 100%, arm 88%, leg 85%.
  • Ranged: an accuracy roll, reduced at close range.
  • Spells: can be resisted. Mana is paid partway through the cast, so an early interruption costs nothing.
  • Block: chance depends on the attack and the defender’s Defense. It needs a one-handed weapon (shield in use) and enough energy.
  • Dodge: a chance to step back from weapon attacks, improved by mobility. Frozen fighters cannot dodge.
  • Damage modifiers: weapon roll, skill, armor matchup, armor type defense, Defense (physical only), wards, and +14% against a staggered target.

Stance, stagger and counters

  • Hits and blocks fill a hidden pressure total; the stance bar shows what is left. The limit rises with Defense.
  • Heavy swings and bashes apply the most pressure. Head hits add 25%; blocked hits apply about half.
  • Stance recovers after 1.35 seconds without a hit.
  • A break staggers the fighter for about 1.2 seconds. Blocking with almost no energy left also staggers.
  • A shield bash can follow a block or interrupt a nearby heavy windup. It has a 2.8 second cooldown.
  • Shield wear: Blocking an axe or mace family melee strike consumes one durability, including on a perfect block. Bucklers break on the first such hit, round shields on the second, and tower shields on the third. The final block stops the hit and breaks the shield. Shield bonuses, blocks and bashes return with a fresh shield next bout.
  • Perfect block: a melee blow stopped by the shield has a 45% chance to be well timed (+15% against heavy swings, more with high Defense, 25–70%). It applies less stance damage and shows PERFECT BLOCK.
  • Riposte bash: a perfect block is followed by a shield bash that ignores the cooldown. If it lands, it knocks the target back about 1.6 units (scaled by shield bash power).
  • Wall slam: knockback that reaches the arena edge staggers the target for about 1 second, or adds 0.5 seconds to a stagger already under way.
  • Bash by shield: Buckler bashes 35% faster at 80% power. Round is the baseline. Tower bashes 18% slower at 140% power (damage, stance damage and knockback).
  • Sand throw: once per bout, a staggered fighter has a 50% chance to throw sand at an attacker winding up a melee blow within 2.7 units. The attacker is blinded for 2 seconds: each of their attacks misses 45% of the time. The stance bar reads Blinded.

Weapon specials

A generated fighter has a 5% chance to carry a special weapon, which replaces a main or backup weapon of the same style (pure mages never roll one). The Main and Backup pickers under Show gear, stats & spells list them, marked ★, under Special weapons. A fighter carries at most one special: picking a second clears the first. Specials reuse their base weapon's model with new colors and small details, and their state shows under the fighter's stance bar.

Improvement opportunities

These come from reading the code. They have not been playtested or balance-simulated. Size is a rough estimate: S, M or L.

Make hits feel bigger

  • Sound (S–M): the game is silent. Add clangs, thuds, spell cues and crowd reactions.
  • Impact (S): add screen shake, longer freezes on big hits, weapon trails and larger damage numbers.
  • Finishers (M): slow motion and a camera push on the killing blow, on top of the finisher animations.
  • Crowd (M): a hype meter that rises with counters, staggers and comebacks.
  • Arena first (M): move gear and stats into a drawer so the fight fills the screen, with health bars close to it.

Make fights more dramatic

  • Signature moves (M): one build-defining move per weapon family, charged by a visible meter.
  • Readable reactions (M): knockdowns and parries for two-handed weapons. Perfect blocks, riposte bashes and knockback are in.
  • Personality (M): traits such as reckless, patient or showman that change decisions.
  • Pacing (S): a sudden-death rule so two defensive builds cannot drag on.

Fix what undercuts choices

  • Attack stat (S): give it an effect on accuracy or block penetration.
  • Sword special case (S): only the plain Sword gets the rush against crossbows. Apply it to other quick weapons.
  • Frost wording (S): Frost Lance says it slows the target, but it slows attacks, not movement. Freeze stops movement, not attacks. Make the behavior and text match.
  • Balance runner (M): simulate thousands of bouts without drawing, then report win rates by weapon, armor and spellbook.
  • Shared data and tests (M–L): separate combat rules from drawing. This page’s numbers are typed by hand and could then be generated.

Decided: fights are fully idle

Once a fight starts, the player cannot interact with it (decided 2026-11-26). New ideas belong in preparation, progression or presentation, not in-fight commands.

  • Stakes (M–L): persistent fighters, saving, rewards, injuries and rivalries.
  • Preparation (M): training, tactics and gear chosen before the fight.
  • Replays (M): use seeded randomness so offline or notable fights can be watched again.

Start with: sound and impact, then saved fighters.
